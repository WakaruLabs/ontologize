\begin{abstract}
Ideally, mechanistic interpretability would provide guarantees about the
computational process used by a model: features should be causal,
composable, and closed-form. Most existing techniques, such as sparse
autoencoders, can perform arbitrarily badly on all three. Arguably the
simplest interpretability technique is a linear probe, and the simplest
control technique is a steering vector. They are remarkably robust
compared to unsupervised techniques such as SAEs, but require labeled
contrastive datasets. We propose a MoEfication
technique that decomposes a representation space into a set of
contrastive classifiers. Each class corresponds to a representative
vector; inputs are encoded as classifications and reconstructed from the
vectors they select. Steering effectively comes for free, outputs are
bounded, the representative vectors admit static interpretation in
principle, and feature space is structured. Preliminary results on SONAR
sentence embeddings and GPT-2 residuals: trained heads are contrast
sets; a hard code of 1900 bits with no continuous coefficients
reconstructs 40\% better than a top-$k$ SAE at matched bits, and on
GPT-2 an 800-bit code recovers more of the model's loss than a $k{=}32$
SAE. Entries are realized by steps in embedding space, and interventions
set on heads compose additively. But an input-space step disturbs other
heads as much as a random step of its length, past the first layer no
head reproduces across seeds, and parameter-space decodes do not
describe what an entry does.
\end{abstract}
