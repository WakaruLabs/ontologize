\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Mechanistic interpretability can currently perform supervised steering
well or unsupervised steering poorly. The Google DeepMind mechanistic
interpretability team has pivoted from ambitious reverse-engineering to
pragmatic interpretability evaluated on proxy tasks, and has deprioritized
sparse autoencoder (SAE) research: SAE probes did not generalize better
out of distribution, and SAEs did not outperform a steering vector built
from a single contrastive pair of prompts at suppressing a model's
awareness of being evaluated~\cite{gdmprogress2025}. On current
benchmarks, supervised difference-of-means vectors steer better than SAE
latents, though still worse than
prompting~\cite{wu2025axbenchsteeringllmssimple}. However, supervised
methods only find concepts for which an experimenter already has labeled
examples, and a direction optimized to produce a desired output change
need not reflect how the model normally computes: a subspace intervention
optimized for its effect can instead activate a dormant
pathway~\cite{makelov2023}.

Steering vectors can be learned without labels and without a dictionary,
by training a perturbation of fixed norm to maximize the change it
produces in a later layer's activations on a single
prompt~\cite{mack2024melbo}. That work reports its vectors largely
qualitatively, with neither a quantitative random-direction baseline nor
a repeat across initializations, each of which, it notes, may reach a
different stationary point. The dominant unsupervised paradigm is the SAE, which approximates a
model's hidden state using a few discrete features drawn from an
overcomplete dictionary, originally with a $\relu$ activation and a
sparsity penalty~\cite{cunningham2023,bricken2023} and more recently with
a $\topk$ activation and no explicit sparsity loss~\cite{gao2024}. The
underlying motivation is to decompile a model into a finite state
machine, yet such a machine is only as interpretable as an enumeration of
its states. Even a mixture of two simple processes (one constant and one
a fair coin) requires infinitely many states to track which component is
active, so the structure lies in how the components
overlap~\cite{crutchfield2026}. Ideally, mechanistic interpretability
would provide guarantees about the computational process that a model
uses, yielding features that are causal, disentangled, and closed-form.
Existing SAE features can fail on all three criteria while remaining
computationally expensive.

\subsection{Problems with SAE features}

\paragraph{Features are not disentangled.}
A representation is disentangled when changing a single underlying factor
of variation changes a single factor of the
representation~\cite{locatello2019}. A persistent problem with SAEs is
feature absorption~\cite{chanin2025}: when a narrow feature implies a
broader one, sparsity favors folding the broad feature's direction into
the narrow latent and leaving the broad latent inactive on those inputs,
so a seemingly monosemantic latent fails to fire on subset cases (for
instance, a ``starts with S'' latent that remains silent on ``short''). A
related failure mode, feature composition~\cite{bussmann2025}, merges
frequently co-occurring concepts into a single latent so that neither can
be modified independently. Nested and hierarchical SAE variants mitigate
both issues (Section~\ref{sec:background}).

\paragraph{Features are not cleanly causal.}
Sparsity trades off against reconstruction fidelity~\cite{gao2024}, and
using SAE features for steering often produces inconsistent,
context-sensitive effects: adding a decoder direction shifts logits
differently across contexts and perturbs unrelated downstream features.
While steerability varies across features and can be partially predicted
before intervening from decoder-direction crowding and direct logit
effects, the most predictive metrics vary across models and
dictionaries~\cite{duan2026}.

\paragraph{Features are not closed-form.}
A major concern is how well an interpretability technique generalizes out
of distribution; ideally, behavior could be inferred from the model's
weights alone. Bilinear MLPs make this partly possible because the
interaction matrix for any output direction can be eigendecomposed
without data, though in deep networks the target output directions still
depend on data-driven SAEs~\cite{pearce2025}.

\paragraph{Forward passes are expensive.}
Although only a handful of features are active for a given input, the
entire feature space must be evaluated for each
sample~\cite{gao2024,mudide2025}. Mixture-of-experts (MoE) SAE variants
reduce this cost by dividing the feature space across smaller submodules
(Section~\ref{sec:background}).

\subsection{Contrastive classifiers}

Less explored is the effect that the structure imposed by an MoE
architecture has on the interpretability of feature space. A common
approach to interpreting features at scale is to have an LLM generate an
explanation from activating examples~\cite{bills2023} and score how well
an LLM judge,
given that explanation alone, distinguishes activating from
non-activating contexts~\cite{paulo2025}. With an unstructured feature
space, it quickly becomes intractable to interpret every combination of
features in this manner, whereas structured expert routings can be
analyzed module by module. The ReLU feed-forward layers of a trained
transformer can themselves be partitioned into experts of co-activating
neurons, retaining over 95\% of task performance while activating only
10--30\% of the parameters~\cite{zhang2022}. Language models also appear
to represent a categorical concept as a simplex whose vertices correspond
to category values, with hierarchically related concepts placed in
orthogonal subspaces so that a hierarchy forms a direct sum of simplices;
differences between vertex vectors then act as steering vectors for
binary contrasts~\cite{park2025}.

We conjecture that MoEfying a representation into classifiers can expose
such latent contrastive steering vectors, because swapping a classifier's
selected entry applies the difference between two entry vectors. Existing
MoE SAEs route similar inputs to the same expert~\cite{mudide2025} and
score an expert as specialized when its features resemble one another and
differ from those of other
experts~\cite{xu2025redundancydiversespecializedmultiexpert}. Contrastive
learning requires the opposite structure, grouping inputs that lie at
opposite ends of a shared axis. The Ontologizer\footnote{Gene Ontology
terms were a source of inspiration for the name, which this work shares
with Ontologizer, a tool for Gene Ontology term enrichment
analysis~\cite{bauer2008}; the two are separate projects.} decomposes a
representation space into a set of such contrastive classifiers. Each
\emph{head} is a $k$-way classifier over its input, and each of its
\emph{entries} corresponds to a representative vector, its \emph{atom}.
An input is encoded as one \emph{assignment} per head and reconstructed
from the atoms that those assignments select across a stack of layers,
each of which takes as input the residual left unexplained by earlier
layers. Interpretability is then expected from \emph{MoEfication} in an
extended sense~\cite{zhang2022}: decomposing the representation space
into small classification tasks, one per head, each nameable in
isolation, where within-head entry swaps act as steering moves.

behavior of the softmax Ontologizer.

\subsection{Disjoint support}
\label{sec:intro-disjoint}

In the concatenated-head variant, each head writes to its own block of
dictionary coordinates, so atoms from different heads have disjoint
supports by construction. Disjoint support is a strong form of
orthogonality: for non-negative atoms the two coincide
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:disjoint}), whereas signed vectors can be orthogonal
through cancellation while sharing every coordinate. When heads are
summed across the full dictionary space, disjoint support can only be
encouraged via a penalty and does not form in practice: no trained summed
model partitions its dictionary coordinates between heads, and penalizing
support overlap on GPT-2 yields a coordinate partition only by shifting
atom energy into directions that the decoder discards or shrinks, where
it does not affect reconstruction (Section~\ref{sec:disjoint},
Appendix~\ref{sec:support}). Enforcing disjoint support by concatenation
affects each of the four SAE failure modes above differently. In every
case, the guarantee holds between heads within a single layer's
dictionary space; outputs still pass through a shared linear decoder,
which in general maps different heads' blocks onto overlapping subspaces.

\paragraph{Disentanglement.}
Feature absorption requires both a general latent that turns off when a
more specific latent fires and a specific latent whose direction absorbs
the general one. Classifier heads prevent the first step because every
head selects an entry on every input and cannot simply turn off, while
disjoint dictionary blocks prevent the second step in dictionary space
because no entry of one head has nonzero weight on another head's
coordinate block. Feature composition is discouraged rather than ruled
out. Because $h$ heads of $k$ entries define $k^h$ combined codes from
$hk$ atoms, independent factors can occupy separate heads, and theory
characterizes when an efficient code separates them. Under a quadratic
activity cost and bounded weights~\cite{whittington2023,dorrell2025}, a
non-negative code assigns two factors disjoint supports when their joint
distribution covers a broad enough product range, roughly when every
combination of their extreme values occurs~\cite{dorrell2025}. Although
that condition applies to the unknown generative factors of the data, our
learned codes are consistent with it: among
entry pairs from two heads that would occur at least ten times under
independence, at most 0.24\% have zero count in any layer of a SONAR
model. Without activity regularization, summed models do not partition
their coordinates (Appendix~\ref{sec:support}), whereas concatenated
heads enforce the partition by construction. Even so, nothing forces a
single head to capture one concept rather than a conjunction of two, and
unsupervised learning from marginal data alone cannot recover underlying
generative factors without matching inductive biases on both the model
and the data~\cite{locatello2019}. Head structure provides one such
architectural bias, and Section~\ref{sec:seeds} measures how far it
extends.

\paragraph{Causality.}
One source of steering collateral in SAEs is crowding among decoder
directions~\cite{duan2026}. With concatenated heads, atoms from different
heads never overlap in dictionary space: forcing a head's entry modifies
only that head's coordinate block, orthogonal to the outputs of all other
heads in the same layer, so interventions on different heads shift the
layer's dictionary representation along orthogonal directions and each
head's contribution can be isolated directly from its block. Summed heads
also compose additively by linearity, but because their dictionary writes
overlap, a single head's contribution cannot be separated from the
others' in the pooled vector. Disjoint dictionary blocks do not, however,
govern how other heads respond when an input is perturbed in input space.
When evaluated on summed models by re-encoding a stepped input, an
input-space step flips as many other heads as a random step of equal
norm, both because heads sit close to their decision boundaries and
because downstream layers reclassify the modified residual
(Section~\ref{sec:entangle}). Disjoint dictionary writes eliminate
neither mechanism.

\paragraph{Closed form.}
Statically interpreting a head from its atoms without data requires heads
to occupy disjoint coordinates as a weight-level property across all
inputs, rather than merely exhibiting low average overlap on a dataset
(Section~\ref{sec:disjoint}). Summed models violate this condition, with
each head's usage spanning 52--98\% of dictionary coordinates.
Concatenated heads satisfy it by construction, and when each head's
decoder block has full column rank, every component of a head's atoms
maps nontrivially to the output, unlike the degenerate partitions
produced by penalizing overlap (Appendices~\ref{sec:support}
and~\ref{sec:concatgeom}). However, this structural property is a
necessary precondition rather than a complete interpretation method:
decoding atom directions through the paired decoder has not yielded
usable descriptions of entries in the models evaluated so far, none of
which used concatenated heads (Section~\ref{sec:autointerp}).

\paragraph{Cost.}
Disjoint support does not reduce encoder compute. Concatenating heads
partitions a dictionary of width $e$ across $h$ heads, so each head
stores $k$ atoms of $e/h$ coordinates rather than $e$ ($h$ times fewer
dictionary parameters at matched $e$), and a hard forward pass in the
dictionary reduces to $h$ slice lookups that assemble a single
$e$-dimensional vector. However, the classifier still evaluates all $hk$
logits per input, just as an SAE evaluates all of its latents, leaving
encoder compute unchanged. Narrow coordinate blocks can also degrade
reconstruction. At $d_{\mathrm{head}}{=}k{=}32$, where each head's block
is only as wide as its $k$ non-negative atoms under constant temperature,
concatenated heads had the highest held-out error among the GPT-2 orthant
arms ($\fvu = 0.416$ vs.\ $0.154$--$0.293$; Table~\ref{tab:orthant}),
because $k$ orthogonal non-negative atoms within a head require one
coordinate each (Appendix~\ref{sec:concatgeom}). Giving each head
additional dimensions ($d_{\mathrm{head}}{=}48 > k{=}32$) and annealing
the temperature brings concatenated heads to an unwhitened $\mathrm{FVU}$
of $0.186$ ($0.164$ at $k{=}64$), outperforming direct atoms at $0.195$
(Section~\ref{sec:gpt2hard}).

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

No single line of prior work combines all elements of this architecture,
though each design choice has close counterparts in neighboring areas.

\paragraph{Quantizers and discrete latents.}
A \texttt{DictBlock} layer ($h$ heads, each computing a softmax over $k$
entries whose atoms are summed) is a soft additive
quantizer~\cite{babenko2014}, generalizing product
quantization~\cite{jegou2011} by allowing every codebook to span the full
space rather than a disjoint block of coordinates (concatenated heads
recover a product quantizer in dictionary space). Composite
quantization~\cite{zhang2014composite} lies between the two, holding the
summed inner products between codewords from different codebooks constant
(where product quantization's disjoint blocks fix them at zero), while
optimized product quantization~\cite{ge2014} learns the rotation that
defines the coordinate blocks. In dictionary-learning terms, a single
hard head performs vector quantization (VQ) with one unit-coefficient
atom, and a sum of heads lies between VQ and unconstrained sparse coding,
selecting exactly one unit-coefficient atom per codebook. Under the
gain--shape split (Eq.~\eqref{eq:y_norm}), each layer acts as gain--shape
VQ whose scalar gain is the residual's observed norm rather than a
quantized index~\cite{aharon2006}. Stacking residual layers yields a
softmax relaxation of multi-stage residual
VQ~\cite{juang1982,barnes1996}, the quantizer used in
neural audio codecs such as SoundStream~\cite{zeghidour2021}, which
selects each stage's codeword by nearest-neighbor search and evaluates
codes solely by bitrate and perceptual quality rather than codeword
interpretability; residual VQ has also been applied directly to SONAR
embeddings using 64 codebooks of 8192
entries~\cite{lcmteam2024largeconceptmodelslanguage}. The
straight-through arm (Section~\ref{sec:ste}) makes this connection
explicit: using a hard argmax in the forward pass and the softmax
gradient in the backward pass---the straight-through
estimator~\cite{bengio2013} in its categorical form~\cite{jang2017}---it
forms a residual additive quantizer with non-negative atoms whose
codewords are selected by learned classifiers rather than
nearest-codeword search. Vectors of categorical variables trained with
straight-through gradients also serve as the latent state of discrete
world models~\cite{hafner2021,hafner2023}, which sample each categorical
variable from the softmax distribution that the backward pass
differentiates rather than taking its argmax (DreamerV3 additionally
mixes in a 1\% uniform prior~\cite{hafner2023}). In those models,
however, the discrete codes serve purely as compressed state and are not
interpreted as features. Codebook Features~\cite{tamkin2023} is the
closest ancestor in mechanistic interpretability: it fine-tunes VQ
bottlenecks into a transformer, placing one codebook per attention head
before the shared output projection (analogous to concatenated heads with
a shared decoder), and steers behavior by substituting codewords,
including swapping one state's codeword for another's on a synthetic
finite-state task. However, its codewords are selected by cosine
similarity to the codebook vectors rather than by a separate classifier,
are interpreted individually as binary present-or-absent features, and
are evaluated solely on target effect without a structured intervention
algebra or a measure of off-target collateral.

\paragraph{Routed and mixture-of-experts dictionaries.}
Switch SAEs~\cite{mudide2025} route each activation to one of several
expert $\topk$ SAEs to reduce training compute. Their router groups
similar inputs together, and experts duplicate some features (5--10\% of
decoder directions have a near-duplicate in another expert), a phenomenon
that Xu et al.\ characterize as experts collapsing into redundant
ensembles and mitigate by activating multiple experts per
input~\cite{xu2025redundancydiversespecializedmultiexpert}. A classifier
head layer adopts the dual arrangement, in which every head is active on
every input and selects a single entry. In the hard-code stack, heads
partition inputs nearly independently (Figure~\ref{fig:headnmi}), whereas
in the reference softmax model, within-head decoded directions repel one
another (Section~\ref{sec:prelim-orth}), so each head forms a contrast
set rather than the cluster of similar features that MoE methods treat as
a specialized expert. Although MoEfication~\cite{zhang2022} motivates our
terminology, its experts are extracted post hoc from pre-trained layers
and multiple experts fire per input; here, entries within a head are
mutually exclusive, and the partitions are learned end to end during
dictionary training.

\paragraph{Deep clustering.}
Each head is an unsupervised $k$-way classifier, connecting our training
objective to deep clustering. Specifically, the batch-mean uniformity
term $\mathrm{KL}_m$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:KL}) mirrors the anti-collapse
regularizer of contrastive clustering~\cite{li2021cc} and
SCAN~\cite{vangansbeke2020scan}, preventing heads from assigning most
inputs to a few entries, while the partition-consensus statistic
$\mathrm{KL}_{\mathrm{pwak}}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:stats}) pulls each
assignment toward a stop-gradient average of its neighbors' assignments,
analogous to DEC's self-training target~\cite{xie2016dec} and SCAN's
neighbor-consistency loss. Whereas deep-clustering methods learn a single
partition of a trainable representation and are evaluated against
ground-truth labels, the Ontologizer trains many heads simultaneously to
partition a frozen representation through reconstruction loss alone, with
the resulting partitions serving directly as the representation code.

\paragraph{Structured SAEs.}
Sparse autoencoders aim to recover features in
superposition~\cite{elhage2022} as sparse linear combinations of
directions from an overcomplete
dictionary~\cite{cunningham2023,bricken2023}; our $\topk$ baselines are
k-sparse autoencoders~\cite{makhzani2014} trained following Gao et
al.~\cite{gao2024}. One major axis of structural prior in recent SAEs is
hierarchical scale: Matryoshka SAEs~\cite{bussmann2025} adapt the
nested-prefix loss of Matryoshka representation
learning~\cite{kusupati2022} to dictionary learning in order to reduce
feature absorption~\cite{chanin2025}, while Tree SAEs~\cite{cao2026}
enforce explicit parent--child structure by gating child latents on
assigned parents. A second axis is subspace grouping: subspace-aware
SAEs~\cite{dalili2026} bundle latents into rank-$r$ blocks and enforce
sparsity across blocks so that each active block spans a continuous
signed subspace, and block-sparse featurizers~\cite{fel2026structuring}
motivate this prior by modeling activations as sums of low-dimensional
manifolds that single-direction sparse codes must otherwise tile with
many separate atoms~\cite{bhalla2026manifolds}. A block-sparse unit is
thus the structural opposite of a classifier head: blocks are selected
sparsely and vary continuously within their subspaces, whereas every head
is active on every input and selects one of $k$ discrete atoms.
OrtSAEs~\cite{korznikov2025} instead penalize the cosine similarity
between each decoder direction and its nearest neighbor. Rung~3 of our
ablation ladder (\texttt{k32\_p5}) incorporates the Matryoshka
nested-prefix loss using five equal-width blocks rather than the
geometrically doubling prefixes of Bussmann et al.~\cite{bussmann2025}, a
logarithmic spacing that Matryoshka representation learning found
superior to uniform spacing~\cite{kusupati2022}. By contrast, the
Ontologizer structures the dictionary by \emph{partitioning features into
exhaustive, mutually exclusive heads}. This design mirrors the geometry
observed in toy models of superposition, where mutually exclusive
features share a low-dimensional subspace with negative internal
interference while independent feature sets occupy mutually orthogonal
subspaces~\cite{elhage2022}; a classifier head enforces within-head
exclusivity by construction and encourages between-head orthogonality
(Section~\ref{sec:disjoint}). Outside dictionary learning, Huang and
Hahn~\cite{huang2025decomposing} decompose a residual stream without
supervision into mutually independent orthogonal subspaces under the
hypothesis that each subspace holds mutually exclusive features, though
their subspaces carry continuous activations rather than discrete $k$-way
selections. Post hoc analyses also show that co-occurring LLM SAE latents
cluster in decoder space by document genre~\cite{li2024geometry},
matching the co-firing structure that \texttt{headstruct.py} recovers
among SONAR SAE latents in place of categorical groups
(Appendix~\ref{sec:categorical}). Each of these structured approaches
tests whether architectural inductive biases yield interpretability
beyond what code density alone explains. In our autointerp evaluations
below, per-latent describability shows no such gain; head structure only
shows a suggestive effect in within-head description coherence on the
single Ontologizer checkpoint evaluated.

\paragraph{Embedding-space substrate.}
Training SAEs on dense text embeddings has recently attracted growing
interest, including interpreting and discretizing dense-retrieval
embeddings~\cite{park2025decodingdenseembeddingssparse}, aligning
sentence-embedding features with human concepts to steer
retrieval~\cite{shin2026aligningsentenceembeddingshuman}, and constructing
interpretable document embeddings from token-level LLM
SAEs~\cite{jiang2026interpretableembeddingssparseautoencoders}. All three
approaches use single-layer $\topk$ or BatchTopK SAEs, with the
embedding-level studies focusing on English retrieval encoders. None
examines SONAR, where sequence-level generation models such as Large
Concept Models operate directly in embedding
space~\cite{lcmteam2024largeconceptmodelslanguage} and where the paired
M2M100 decoder maps reconstructions and interventions back into text
without requiring a separately trained inverter. Inverting pooled
sentence embeddings otherwise requires training a dedicated decoder that
recovers exact text only by iteratively re-embedding and correcting its
candidates~\cite{morris2023textembeddingsrevealalmost}, as exact
training-free inversion is known only from per-token causal language
model activations~\cite{nikolaou2025} rather than bidirectional
mean-pooled embeddings. Because \texttt{textfid.py} and
\texttt{steerfid.py} decode in a single greedy pass through a generative
decoder that paraphrases and is sensitive to small embedding
perturbations~\cite{sonar,lcmteam2024largeconceptmodelslanguage}, our
text-space fidelity scores represent conservative lower bounds
(Appendix~\ref{sec:steertext}).

\paragraph{Evaluation stance.}
SAEBench~\cite{karvonen2025} motivates evaluating dictionaries across
diverse downstream criteria rather than solely by reconstruction at fixed
sparsity. From its eight metrics, our evaluation suite adapts three to SONAR (a
downstream fidelity analogue through the paired decoder,
\texttt{textfid.py}; LLM-judged feature detection, \texttt{autointerp.py};
and $k_{\mathrm{probe}}$-sparse probing over 86 language labels,
\texttt{langprobe.py}) while omitting absorption, RAVEL, unlearning, SCR,
and TPP. For automated interpretability, we adopt
the detection protocol of Paulo et al.~\cite{paulo2025}; however, because
we evaluate positive examples ranked immediately below the prompt-building
examples and report F1 rather than balanced accuracy, our numerical
scores are not directly comparable to theirs. Following
MDL-SAEs~\cite{ayonrinde2024}, we compare model capacity in bits rather
than $\mathrm{L0}$, since an $\mathrm{L0}$ axis favors arbitrarily wide
dictionaries with fewer active latents; their description-length metric
(summed per-latent usage entropy) corresponds to the realized-bits
estimator that we apply to heads (Eq.~\eqref{eq:realized}). On
AxBench~\cite{wu2025axbenchsteeringllmssimple}, prompting and fine-tuning
outperform all representation-based steering methods across four Gemma-2
sites. Among single-direction methods, only supervised difference of
means outperforms SAE latents, whereas linear probes match the strongest
concept detectors yet steer poorly, and SAE latents selected via
autointerp descriptions lag behind on concept detection. This dissociation
between detection and steering motivates evaluating dictionary directions
directly by their causal effects. Accordingly, \texttt{steerfid.py}
measures target effect against off-target collateral at matched
perturbation magnitude, following Cunningham et al.'s evaluation of
feature edits across intervention magnitudes~\cite{cunningham2023} rather
than tuning per-method steering strengths on held-out prompts as in
AxBench. Song et
al.~\cite{song2025positionmechanisticinterpretabilityprioritize} further
argue that cross-seed feature consistency should be a core SAE evaluation
criterion, quantified via the mean absolute cosine similarity of
one-to-one matched decoder directions across seeds; our Hungarian-matched
head NMI (\texttt{partition.py}) provides the partition-level analogue
for classifier heads, while activation-containment match fractions
(\texttt{splitting.py}) provide a thresholded activation-based analogue.
Wang et al.~\cite{wang2024} similarly measure cross-run consistency via
each latent's maximum activation correlation with latents from a second
run relative to model-seed and SAE-seed skylines. Paulo and
Belrose~\cite{paulo2025seeds} find that two $\topk$ SAEs trained on the
same data in the same order from different initializations share only
42\% of their latents (matched one to one on both encoder and decoder
directions at cosine $\ge 0.7$), fewer as the dictionary widens, so seed
dependence is not peculiar to classifier heads. Korznikov et
al.~\cite{korznikov2026} show that SAEs trained with the decoder frozen
at a random initialization or held near it, or with the encoder frozen at
random, score within a few percentage
points of fully trained SAEs on autointerp, single-latent sparse probing,
and RAVEL, and Heap et al.~\cite{heap2025} find that SAEs on randomly
re-initialized transformers reach autointerp scores close to those on
trained ones in the larger models they test, the two being separated
instead by how token-specific the latents are; our permutation nulls shuffle a trained model's own entries,
latents, or sample rows, and therefore do not isolate how much training
improves over random projections in those metrics. Decoding parameter
vectors through SONAR's text decoder to test weight-only interpretability
serves as a closed-form instance of dataset-free feature synthesis, an
approach that struggles even on planted ground-truth triggers, helping
human evaluators identify triggers less than half the
time~\cite{casper2023}; parameter-only decodes fail in our setting as
well. Two elements of our framework have, to our knowledge, no close
counterpart in prior work: treating per-head routing as contrastive classification equipped
with a direct causal intervention interface, and testing within-head and
within-group coherence against size-matched random subsets from the same
dictionary across both weight geometry and LLM-generated descriptions (by
contrast, Li et al.~\cite{li2024geometry} test global co-occurrence
clusterings of SAE latents against permuted labels).

\section{Evaluation frame}
\label{sec:frame}

Many of the comparisons below follow standard SAE evaluation criteria
(per-feature monosemanticity, sparse probing, and reconstruction at
matched sparsity), and we report those measurements unchanged. However,
the goal of the Ontologizer is not to learn a monosemantic dictionary of
independent latents, but rather to serve as an \emph{unsupervised
generator of steering vectors}. Each head is trained solely via
reconstruction loss with the aim of partitioning the representation space
into contrastive categories, where within-head entry swaps provide the
steering primitive: forcing head $i$ from its argmax entry to entry $j'$
shifts the decoded output along the axis that head $i$ classifies.

Under this framing, our evaluation addresses four primary questions:
(a)~what reconstruction penalty contrast-set structure incurs (the Pareto
frontier and structural ablation ladder);
(b)~whether trained heads behave as contrastive classifiers in practice
(categorical selectivity and within-head semantic repulsion);
(c)~whether the learned partitions capture \emph{natural} factors that
are interpretable, reproducible across runs, and aligned with generative
factors of the data (cross-seed reproducibility, language, and topic
alignment); and
(d)~whether entry swaps provide effective steering with consistent target
effects and low collateral, comparable to supervised difference-of-means
vectors and SAE decoder-direction addition (steering fidelity). Metrics
that test whether \emph{individual latents} align with isolated concepts
remain informative for the SAE baselines, but are ill-suited to
evaluating classifier codes; we note this distinction wherever such
metrics appear. Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} illustrates the softmax
Ontologizer on three criteria under question~(d), whereas the hard code
