\section{Preliminary results}
\label{sec:prelim}
Our evaluation is ongoing and the results so far are mixed. This section
summarizes the headline result for each line of work; full experimental
protocols, ablations, and secondary analyses are reported in the working
notes\footnote{K.~Wiechecki et al., \emph{Dictionary
learning as unsupervised steering-vector generation: Ontologizers vs.\
sparse autoencoders on SONAR sentence embeddings}, working notes, in
preparation; distributed with this proposal.}.

Three model families appear throughout the SONAR comparisons:
\begin{itemize}
	\item the softmax Ontologizer \texttt{resid\_nc}, which stacks
	$l{=}5$ layers of $h{=}32$ heads with $k{=}32$ entries (a soft code);
	\item the straight-through Ontologizer \texttt{ste\_h76\_init01},
	which stacks $5\times76$ heads at $k{=}32$ (a 1900-bit hard code); and
	\item $\topk$ SAEs trained on the same embedding cache under the same
	whitened MSE objective.
\end{itemize}
The SONAR cache contains embeddings from an 86-language mC4 sample. Due
to a tagging bug in the original cache, 93\% of rows were encoded with
SONAR's English source-language tag rather than their own language tag;
fixing the tag shifts individual embeddings by roughly twice the hard
code's reconstruction error, but changes the share of variance explained
by language only slightly (from 7.4\% to 6.7\%). Reconstruction is
scored by $\mathrm{FVU}_w$ on the final 32{,}768 rows of the cache,
which are held out from training for all SAEs and straight-through
models. Although the softmax Ontologizers were trained before that split
was introduced, re-evaluating them on freshly encoded out-of-sample rows
changes their $\mathrm{FVU}_w$ by at most 0.3\%.

\subsection{The price of a soft code}
\label{sec:prelim-pareto}
Imposing contrast-set structure on a soft code carries a steep
reconstruction penalty. Across all capacities tested, standard $\topk$
SAEs dominate the softmax Ontologizer's truncated deviation code, which
retains the $\nu$ entries per head furthest from the mean assignment and
pins the rest to the mean (Table~\ref{tab:prelim-pareto}). At
$\mathrm{FVU}_w \approx 0.49$, an SAE transmits 32 active coefficients
whereas the Ontologizer requires 320, and at matched capacity the
unstructured SAE reconstructs roughly $50\times$ more accurately.
Furthermore, taking the one-hot argmax of the softmax model fails to
reconstruct the input at all, yielding $\mathrm{FVU}_w = 17.34$. That is
$77\times$ higher than the Gaussian rate--distortion bound at 800 bits
($0.226$), which upper-bounds the optimal distortion achievable on any
source with the same covariance~\cite{cover2006}. In a soft model, the
discrete argmax entries that an intervention forces are therefore not what
carries the reconstruction, which motivates training hard codes directly.

\subsection{Trained atoms form contrast sets}
\label{sec:prelim-orth}
Within each head, the learned atoms exhibit the negative cosine
similarity expected of a contrast set. In 96\% of the softmax
Ontologizer's heads, the decoded atoms have lower pairwise cosine
similarity than size-matched random subsets of atoms drawn from the same
dictionary (Table~\ref{tab:prelim-headcoh}), and this repulsion is even
stronger under the whitened inner product used in training. Thus, a
soft head's entries represent separated alternatives rather than a
cluster of similar directions. The hard-code stack shows a similar
pattern in its weights, with within-head decoded atoms roughly orthogonal
to slightly negatively correlated (mean cosine $0.023$ in layer 0 and
$-0.015$ to $-0.020$ in deeper layers). Because each head's assignments
sum to one, however, adding a constant vector to one head's atoms and
subtracting it from another head in the same layer leaves the layer's
output unchanged; only differences between atoms within a head are
uniquely determined by the function, and a centered-atom null comparison
remains to be run. By contrast, \texttt{g160top1}---which applies hard
per-head selection in a single layer with unconstrained per-head
magnitudes---learns positively correlated atoms within each head.

Between heads, however, the intended coordinate partition does not form
in summed models. Across all summed models tested, the support overlap
$\omega$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:support}) remains at or above a coordinate-shuffled
null, with each head's usage spanning 52--98\% of dictionary coordinates
rather than the ${\sim}3\%$ expected of a 32-way partition. This overlap
persists in the subspace visible to the decoder, and directly penalizing
$\omega$ on GPT-2 reduces dictionary-space overlap only by pushing
73--95\% of atom energy into the decoder's null or near-null space while
decoded overlap stays 8--10\% above the null (working notes). Summed
heads therefore learn within-head contrast sets that share dictionary
coordinates, whereas concatenated heads enforce a coordinate partition
by construction.

\subsection{A discrete code beats $\topk$ SAEs up to 160 latents, not every sparse code}
\label{sec:prelim-ste}
With straight-through selection, the forward pass uses the discrete
argmax directly. At 1900 index bits, the $5\times76$ hard code achieves
$\mathrm{FVU}_w = 0.154$ using only four scalar gains beyond its discrete
choices, and pinning those gains to their training-set means yields a
purely discrete 1900-bit code at $\mathrm{FVU}_w = 0.158$
(Table~\ref{tab:prelim-ste}). By comparison, a sparse code that also
transmits continuous coefficients must divide its bit budget between
support indices and quantized magnitudes, and no quantization of an SAE's
coefficients can improve on an unconstrained least-squares refit on its
active support. A $k_{\mathrm{SAE}}{=}160$ $\topk$ SAE spends 1206 bits to
index its active set, leaving 4.3 bits per coefficient at a 1900-bit
budget, and even its unquantized support refit has $\mathrm{FVU}_w = 0.236$;
at a matched 1900-bit budget, the pure hard code therefore reduces error
by at least 33\%.

However, SAEs with variable sparsity or wider supports can outperform the
hard stack under entropy coding. When coefficients are quantized and each
latent's symbols are entropy-coded, a weakly penalized ReLU+L1 SAE
reaches $\mathrm{FVU}_w = 0.129$ at an average rate of 1900 bits per
row, because skewed latent firing frequencies reduce index entropy and
allow variable-length codes across rows (whereas the hard code uses
entries nearly uniformly at a fixed 1900 bits per row). When every row is
capped at 1900 bits, the L1 SAE reaches $0.152$ (comparable to the hard
code's $0.158$), and under fixed-length support indexing the hard code
leads ($0.158$ vs.\ $0.180$). On the sentence-split SONAR cache, a wider
$\topk$ SAE ($k_{\mathrm{SAE}}{=}256$ active out of 11{,}264 latents)
beats the hard stacks under both average-rate and per-row budgets
($0.128$ and $0.129$ vs.\ $0.161$ and $0.164$), trailing only when restricted
to a single global quantization step without per-row bit allocation
($0.185$ vs.\ $0.164$). Relative to the Gaussian rate--distortion bound
at 1900 bits, the hard stack's distortion remains $3.1\times$ higher,
reflecting the gap between single-sample quantization and high-dimensional
block coding~\cite{cover2006}.

\subsection{Depth helps reconstruction and costs addressability}
\label{sec:prelim-depth}
Comparing a five-layer stack of 76 heads per layer against a single layer
of 380 heads isolates the effect of residual depth at matched bit budget
(1900 bits), parameter count, and near-uniform entry usage. The five-layer
stack achieves $1.79\times$ lower reconstruction error ($\mathrm{FVU}_w$
$0.1535$ vs.\ $0.2750$). Whereas a single layer of heads acts like a
parallel additive quantizer~\cite{babenko2014} in which heads select
atoms independently without observing one another's residuals (which is
benign only when heads occupy orthogonal subspaces as in product
quantization~\cite{jegou2011}), the residual stack conditions each layer
on the error left by previous layers, as in multi-stage residual
quantization~\cite{zeghidour2021}, while also applying deep supervision
to each prefix.

However, residual depth makes individual heads harder to target via
input-space perturbations. Stepping in embedding space by
$0.25\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert$ along a target entry's decoded atom flips
the target head to that entry on 99.6\% of rows in the single-layer model
while flipping 33\% of other heads, whereas in the five-layer stack it
realizes the target entry on 37\% of rows while flipping 66\% of other
heads. In both models, stepping along a decoded atom flips as many
unrelated heads as a random step of the same norm, both because the hard
classifiers sit close to their decision boundaries (median logit margin
$1.5\times10^{-4}$ between winner and runner-up) and because changes to
an early layer alter the residual seen by all subsequent layers. Among
our hard-selection models, only the single-layer linear variant
\texttt{g160top1} achieves selective input-space addressing, realizing
58\% of targets while flipping 2.6\% of other heads (vs.\ 23\% for a
random step).

\subsection{Steering}
\label{sec:prelim-steer}
Evaluated directly in embedding space, soft-model steering reliably
realizes target entries: stepping by $0.25\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert$ along a
target entry's decoded atom switches the head's argmax to that entry on
82\% of rows, compared to 2\% for a random step of equal norm (an effect
previously obscured by text round-tripping, since decoding and
re-encoding an unsteered embedding preserves a head's argmax on only
11--13\% of rows). When interventions are applied directly to head
assignments, two-head edits compose nearly additively in decode space,
with a median cosine of at least $0.95$ between the joint decode change
and the sum of the individual changes across all layers (and exact
equality in the final layer, where no downstream layers reclassify the
residual). In \texttt{resid\_nc}, forcing a single layer-0 entry
consistently translates decoded text into Burmese while preserving
semantic content, providing a concrete existence proof of a causal
steering entry on one seed.

When we compare decoded-atom steps against supervised vectors fitted to
the model's own entry partitions at step size
$0.25\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert$, the softmax stack's decoded atoms reach
their target entry on 75\% of rows (vs.\ 46--47\% for difference-of-means
and logistic-probe vectors), whereas the hard stack's decoded atoms reach
37\% (vs.\ 91--94\% for the supervised vectors). However, two
arbitrary-partition baselines show that high target realization in
embedding space is a generic property of partitioning a vector space
rather than evidence that the learned partitions capture meaningful
factors. In an untrained $5\times76$ stack, parametric decoded-atom steps
realize targets at chance (2\%), but supervised difference-of-means and
probe vectors reach 75\% and 94\% (and classifier gradients reach 61\%
and 85\%, vs.\ 30\% and 16\% in the trained stack) with one third the
collateral flips of the trained stack (22\% vs.\ 66\%). Similarly, in a
single layer of 380 random-projection heads ($k{=}32$), empirical
cell-centroid swaps realize 78\% of targets ($67\%$ at native step size)
and projection directions, margin gradients, and logistic probes reach
87--100\% at 30--32\% collateral, matching the trained single-layer hard
model. On an external ground-truth attribute---language steering under
matched intervention budgets (Table~\ref{tab:steer-overlay})---forcing a
soft-model entry moves decoded text $0.64\times$ as far along the
supervised difference-of-means axis as supervised steering itself and
$4\times$ as far as a linear probe, though all methods (including
supervised steering) incur collateral content changes comparable to a
random perturbation of the same norm.

\subsection{The error reproduces; the heads do not}
\label{sec:prelim-seed}
Two hard-code runs initialized with different random seeds converge to
the same held-out error ($\mathrm{FVU}_w = 0.1535$ on both seeds), but
their individual heads do not match: Hungarian-matched head partitions
have a mean NMI of $0.012$ (against a $0.005$ permutation null) and
matched head output contributions have a centered cosine similarity of
$0.007$ (null $0.002$), whereas comparing a single run against an earlier
checkpoint 19{,}500 steps prior yields $0.38$ partition NMI and
$0.98$--$0.99$ atom cosine similarity. Even the overall reconstructions
diverge across seeds: the two hard-code seeds' whitened residuals
correlate at $0.30$, compared to $0.83$ between two seeds of a
$k_{\mathrm{SAE}}{=}32$ SAE. Thus, heads stabilize within a training run
but converge to different decompositions across seeds. The same pattern
holds on GPT-2, where only layer-0 heads show above-chance cross-seed
agreement (at less than a fifth of the within-run control), and in the
softmax Ontologizer, where at most 0.1\% of entries match across seeds
compared to 3--31\% for a $k_{\mathrm{SAE}}{=}32$ SAE pair.

Because layer 0 is the only layer whose input is not conditioned on
earlier layers' discrete choices, it is the only layer where individual
heads recur without additional supervision: in every SONAR run, the
largest-norm layer-0 head partitions inputs by broad topic and document
genre (such as crime news, geopolitics, encyclopedic articles,
manufacturing, marketing, legislation, and entertainment, with blinded
contrastive descriptions detected at F1 $0.52$ vs.\ a $0.27$ null).
By contrast, source language---the primary labeled attribute in the
corpus---is distributed across multi-head neighborhoods rather than
isolated in a single head, as expected for a language-agnostic embedding
space. Achieving cross-seed reproducibility across a wider set of heads
requires an explicit paired-view training signal
(Section~\ref{sec:prelim-slow}).

\subsection{Paired sentences make a block of heads reproduce}
\label{sec:prelim-slow}
To test whether temporal or document-level consistency can break the
symmetry across seeds, we trained a single-layer model of 380 heads
($k{=}32$) on pairs of sentences drawn from the same document in a
44.1M-segment sentence-split SONAR cache (\texttt{mc4\_sent16}), adding a
Fano lower bound on the mutual information between the entries assigned
to the two sentences for the first 38 ``slow'' heads. After three epochs,
these 38 slow heads maintain the same entry across adjacent sentences at
Cohen's kappa $0.46$, compared to $0.07$ in an unregularized control.
Across three random seeds, the slow blocks learn matching partitions:
one-to-one Hungarian matching yields a mean NMI of $0.412$--$0.414$ for
every pair of seeds (against a $0.005$ null), every slow head in one seed
pairs with a slow head in the other (chance 10\%), and within-language
agreement across 84 languages remains at NMI $0.312$--$0.314$ (vs.\
$0.041$ null), whereas the remaining 342 unregularized heads and the
control model's heads match at only $0.016$--$0.017$. Moreover, the top 38 slow directions extracted from the
one-hot slow-head code achieve a higher held-out cross-sentence
correlation than the top 38 linear slow-feature directions of the raw
embedding ($0.72$--$0.73$ vs.\ $0.636$ overall, and $0.52$ vs.\ $0.46$
after demeaning each language). The agreement holds for the block rather
than for individual heads: two slow heads within one seed already agree
at NMI $0.40$, any one-to-one pairing of two seeds' slow heads scores
$0.397$--$0.399$, and the three seeds' pairwise matchings compose for only
6 of the 38 heads (1 expected by chance), so no individual slow head is
yet identified across runs. The result is further limited to a one-layer
model and costs $1.8$--$1.9\%$ in $\mathrm{FVU}_w$, and the redundant slow
heads align primarily with the top one or two linear slow directions,
leaving open how many distinct document-level variables the block
captures.

\subsection{Hard codes on GPT-2}
\label{sec:prelim-gpt2}
On GPT-2 small layer-8 residuals, a five-layer 800-bit hard code with
signed direct atoms and no decoder recovers a larger fraction of the
model's next-token cross-entropy loss when spliced into the forward pass
than a $k_{\mathrm{SAE}}{=}32$ $\topk$ SAE, both on the 10M-token
training cache ($97.0\%$ vs.\ $96.2\%$ loss recovered, despite a higher
unwhitened $\mathrm{FVU}$ of $0.217$ vs.\ $0.189$) and after one pass
over 40M tokens ($97.6\%$ at $\mathrm{FVU}$ $0.195$;
Table~\ref{tab:prelim-gpt2}). When the SAE's 32 active coefficients are
quantized to ${\sim}5$ bits each (556--588 total bits vs.\ $800 + {\sim}25$
for the hard code), however, the SAE achieves lower $\mathrm{FVU}$ per
bit; across model families, raw $\mathrm{FVU}$ does not monotonically
predict downstream loss recovery. Replacing the shared decoder in the
40M-token concatenated-head model with separate per-layer decoders
reduces $\mathrm{FVU}$ from $0.186$ to $0.169$ ($97.3\% \to 97.7\%$ loss
recovered) and reduces cross-layer coupling between co-indexed heads from
97\% to 6\% (chance 3\%, though a width-matched shared-decoder control
has not yet been run). Training continuous low-rank fibers on top of the
frozen discrete code recovers $99.9\%$ of the loss without changing the
discrete assignments, at the cost of 640 continuous values per token.

Describability and steering diagnostics on these GPT-2 codes highlight
two important limitations. First, blinded LLM-judge balanced accuracy for
entry descriptions (chance $0.50$; document-split nulls $0.496$--$0.507$)
falls off rapidly after the first layer: layer 0 averages $0.70$--$0.71$
($0.91$ on highest-margin examples, $0.65$ on random members, and $0.55$
near the boundary), layer 1 drops to $0.56$--$0.57$, and layers 2--4 sit
at chance ($0.50$--$0.52$). Moreover, in layer 0 a lookup baseline that
uses only the current token's identity outperforms the LLM judge on
single entries ($0.75$ vs.\ $0.69$ on concatenated heads and $0.74$ vs.\
$0.71$ on direct atoms), and the $+0.10$ accuracy gain from conditioning
on two-head pairs in the concatenated model comes largely from lexical
selectivity ($+0.01$ on direct atoms). Second, while single-head edits
perturb next-token predictions modestly (setting a layer-0 head to its
mean entry costs $0.02$--$0.075$ nats vs.\ $0.055$ nats for zeroing an
active SAE latent, and entry swaps cost $0.001$--$0.06$ nats) and
swapping a head to entry $j'$ shifts next-token probabilities toward
$j'$'s empirical distribution (mean target rank $3.5$--$6$ out of 31 at
unit step and $5$--$8$ at $4\times$ step, vs.\ $14$--$18$ for random
directions), centroid steps between random-projection bins achieve equally
specific next-token steering across all five layers (mean rank
$0.21$--$1.69$ vs.\ $0.56$--$1.37$ for learned entries) because the
downstream map to the logits is locally nearly linear.

\subsection{Automated interpretability}
\label{sec:prelim-autointerp}
Standard per-latent autointerp metrics evaluate individual directions in
isolation rather than contrastive partitions, and across six models
blinded detection F1 tracks log activation density (correlation $-0.89$)
rather than architecture, with the Ontologizer lying on the same
density trend as the SAEs. Much of the apparent advantage of sparse SAEs
on the multilingual cache comes from single-language features: latents
whose top activating examples come from a single language can be
described simply by naming that language, achieving detection F1 of
$0.90$--$0.97$ against random negative examples, and restricting the
comparison to features with corpus-typical language diversity narrows the
sparse SAE's F1 lead over the Ontologizer from $0.17$ to $0.10$. Finally,
generating descriptions by decoding atom directions from parameters alone
without activating examples fails across all models (F1 $0.04$ for the
Ontologizer and at most $0.14$ for any model, below the $0.67$
always-positive baseline), confirming that a single decoded atom vector
does not suffice to name a category without activation examples.

\subsection{Against the desiderata}
We can summarize where the architecture stands relative to the three
desiderata in the introduction:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Causal:} Qualified positive. Target entries can be
	realized by embedding-space steps and used to steer decoded outputs,
	but stepping in input space flips unrelated heads as often as a
	random step of the same norm (compounded by the residual stack), and
	on both SONAR and GPT-2 swaps between arbitrary random-projection or
	untrained cells steer toward their own empirical distributions just
	as specifically as learned entries.
	\item \textbf{Disentangled:} Positive for direct code-level
	interventions, where changing a head's assignment modifies only that
	head and composes additively with edits to other heads.
	\item \textbf{Closed-form:} Negative so far. Parameter-only decodes
	fail to produce valid feature descriptions, and summed models do not
	partition dictionary coordinates across heads without explicit
	concatenation.
\end{itemize}
Underlying all three desiderata is cross-seed identifiability, which the
original proposal took for granted: past the first layer, independent
training runs learn different heads, and only a block of heads trained
with a paired-sentence objective has reproduced across seeds so far, as
a block rather than head by head (Section~\ref{sec:prelim-slow}). On reconstruction fidelity, however,
moving from soft to straight-through hard selection closes the gap with
moderate-width $\topk$ SAEs: the 1900-bit hard code outperforms $\topk$
SAEs up to 160 active latents at matched total bits on SONAR (though not
wider or entropy-coded variable-rate SAEs) and recovers more next-token
