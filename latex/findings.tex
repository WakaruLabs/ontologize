\begin{abstract}
% flockpaper sets the abstract in an unbreakable minipage, so it must fit
% on the page it starts on or it moves to the next one whole
\small
Auditing a model for a hidden objective or an unnamed concept requires
identifying its internal variables without labels. Supervised probes and
difference-of-means vectors outperform sparse autoencoder (SAE) features at
detection and steering, but supervised methods only test concepts that an
experimenter already suspects. We argue that unsupervised SAEs struggle in
part because of the form of their features: each latent either fires or stays silent,
absorption makes silence uninformative, and effective steering vectors
instead contrast alternative values of a variable. The Ontologizer learns
categorical variables directly via stacked layers of competitive classifier
heads, each assigning every input to one of $k$ entries associated with a
learned atom. Forcing a head to another entry swaps one atom for another, an
unsupervised analogue of a difference-of-means vector, so steering can test a
discovered variable, though only against arbitrary partitions of the same
space, which steer their own cells just as specifically on both SONAR and
GPT-2. Once the code is discrete, categorical
structure incurs little distortion: on SONAR sentence embeddings, a pure
1900-bit hard code reaches $\fvu = 0.158$, whereas a 160-latent $\topk$ SAE
at the same bit budget cannot beat $0.236$ (though entropy-coded SAEs with
variable or wider supports achieve lower error); on GPT-2 small, an 800-bit
hard code recovers 97.6\% of next-token loss, surpassing a
$k_{\mathrm{SAE}}{=}32$ SAE at similar error. In the soft code, forced
entries steer along the supervised difference-of-means direction
$0.64\times$ as far as supervised steering and compose nearly additively.
However, the learned variables are not yet identifiable: independent seeds
match in error to four digits while sharing no heads past the first layer,
input-space steps flip other heads as often as random steps of equal norm,
and parameter-only decodes fail to describe entries. The one exception is a
block of heads rewarded for maintaining consistent entries across two
sentences of a document, which reproduces as a block across three seeds (matched
NMI $0.41$ vs.\ a $0.005$ null), though not yet head by head, and varies
more slowly than any linear projection of the embedding. Identifiability
rather than fidelity is therefore the binding constraint for auditing and
stable goal specification: reconstruction loss alone does not enforce it,
and paired-view training is the first objective that improves it.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Data, models, and evaluation}
\label{sec:setup}

This section summarizes our experimental setup; Appendix~\ref{app:methods}
provides the full protocols and defining equations for every evaluation.

\paragraph{Data.}
Our main SONAR models are trained on a fixed cache of 3{,}974{,}400
unit-norm SONAR sentence embeddings ($d{=}1024$) drawn from an interleaved
86-language mC4 sample, with a ground-truth language label per row and
source text recoverable by row index. In this cache, 80 of the 86 languages (93\% of rows) were encoded with
SONAR's English source tag rather than their native tag; a companion
sentence-split cache (\texttt{mc4\_sent16}, 44.1M consecutive segments)
stores unnormalized embeddings encoded under native language tags
(Appendix~\ref{app:data}). Sections~\ref{sec:gpt2} and~\ref{sec:seeds}
additionally evaluate residual-stream activations of GPT-2
small~\cite{radford2019} leaving block 8 of 12 ($d{=}768$), a standard
testbed for sparse dictionary learning~\cite{gao2024}, while
Section~\ref{sec:gpt2hard} uses a separate GPT-2 setup whose results are
compared only within that section (Appendix~\ref{app:data}).

\paragraph{Objective and score.}
All models minimize the whitened MSE of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mse}, and we report
whitened fraction of variance unexplained ($\fvu$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:fvu}) on
the final 32{,}768 rows of the cache, which are held out from training for
all SAEs and straight-through models. Coordinate~837, which is fixed on
raw SONAR embeddings by the final LayerNorm hyperplane, encodes
$1/\lVert\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{raw}}\rVert$ after L2 normalization and
accounts for at most $0.41\%$ of whitened error in any sparse model
(Appendix~\ref{app:data}). Although the softmax Ontologizers were trained
before that holdout split was introduced, re-evaluating them on freshly
encoded out-of-sample rows changes their $\fvu$ by at most 0.3\%
(Appendix~\ref{app:data}). Every evaluation uses each run's final training
temperature.

\paragraph{Models.}
The reference softmax Ontologizer (\texttt{resid\_nc}) stacks $l{=}5$
residual layers of $h{=}32$ heads with $k{=}32$ entries each; a
selection-rule sweep varies only its per-head selection operator, and the
straight-through arm (Section~\ref{sec:ste}) replaces soft assignments
with discrete one-hot codes. Our baselines are $\topk$ SAEs following
Gao et al.~\cite{gao2024}, alongside a structural-ablation ladder that
introduces Ontologizer constraints one step at a time. Two rungs of that
ladder---\texttt{g160top1} and \texttt{g160softmax}, which apply hard or
softmax competition across 160 heads over a shared decoder
(\texttt{g160top1} fusing a scalar router into its classifier)---are
single-layer Ontologizers without bilinear classifiers, residual stacking,
or non-negative dictionaries, and are treated as simplified model variants
rather than external baselines (Appendix~\ref{sec:models}; full
configurations in Appendix~\ref{app:configs}).

\paragraph{Evaluations.}
Each evaluation is implemented as a standalone script run on the shared
evaluation split (Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}):
\begin{itemize}
\item reconstruction error against code capacity and a Gaussian
rate--distortion baseline (\texttt{pareto.py}), and coefficient shrinkage
via least-squares refitting on frozen active supports (\texttt{refit.py});
\item post hoc discovery of exhaustive, mutually exclusive latent groups
in SAEs (\texttt{headstruct.py}), and within-head semantic coherence of
decoded atoms against size-matched random subsets (\texttt{headcoh.py});
\item cross-model feature splitting and cross-seed head matching by sample
partition and by output contribution (\texttt{splitting.py},
\texttt{partition.py}, \texttt{headcontrib.py});
\item downstream text fidelity through the M2M100 decoder
(\texttt{textfid.py}), and language probing by active latents and head
partitions (\texttt{langprobe.py}, \texttt{headlang.py});
\item steering efficacy and collateral in decoded text and in embedding
space (\texttt{steerfid.py}, \texttt{steerembed.py},
\texttt{steergeom.py}), along with additivity of paired head interventions
(\texttt{compose.py}); and
\item automated interpretability detection (\texttt{autointerp.py}).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Reproducibility}

% sloppypar: the paragraph is mostly unbreakable script names, so its lines
% are set loose (five underfull warnings) rather than into the margin
\begin{sloppypar}
All code is available at \texttt{https://github.com/WakaruLabs/ontologize}.
The core evaluation protocols are implemented as root-level scripts
(\texttt{sae.py}, \texttt{pareto.py}, \texttt{headstruct.py},
\texttt{headcoh.py}, \texttt{splitting.py}, \texttt{textfid.py},
\texttt{langprobe.py}, \texttt{refit.py}, \texttt{steerfid.py},
\texttt{compose.py}, \texttt{autointerp.py}), orchestrated by the
\texttt{sae\_ladder.sh}, \texttt{sae\_evals.sh}, and
\texttt{autointerp\_run.sh} runners as idempotent, crash-resumable systemd
user units with rate-metered judge calls. The straight-through arm and its
diagnostics reside in \texttt{experiments/ste-arm/}
(\texttt{train\_ste.py}, \texttt{steerembed.py}, \texttt{blendablate.py},
\texttt{gainablate.py}, \texttt{inputgeom.py}, \texttt{dictgeom.py},
\texttt{lastlayer.py}, \texttt{partition.py}, \texttt{headcontrib.py},
\texttt{entryshare.py}, \texttt{bigatoms.py}, \texttt{headlang.py},
\texttt{headscript.py}, \texttt{headeta.py}, \texttt{embedgeom.py},
\texttt{decodehead.py}, \texttt{coordfvu.py}, \texttt{make\_zca.py}), with
every run's command and superseded analyses recorded in
\texttt{experiments/ste-arm/notes.md}. The sentence-split cache and
paired-sentence experiments reside in \texttt{experiments/sentence-split/}
and \texttt{experiments/next-sentence/} (\texttt{eval\_next.py},
\texttt{transitions.py}, \texttt{train\_pair.py}). The GPT-2 hard-code
experiments of Section~\ref{sec:gpt2hard} reside in
\texttt{experiments/gpt2/} (\texttt{train\_onto.py}, \texttt{diagnose.py},
\texttt{seedstab.py}, with notes, configurations, diagnostics, and loss
curves in \texttt{DIRECT\_GAIN\_STE.md} and \texttt{results/README.md})
and use the \texttt{headline} branch. A curated test suite verifies each
protocol's statistics on synthetic inputs with planted structure.
Evaluation artifacts are stored under \texttt{data/out/sonar/} and
\texttt{data/out/gpt2\_l8/}. At most three straight-through training runs
fit concurrently on a single 24\,GB GPU.
\end{sloppypar}
