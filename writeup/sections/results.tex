Findings. (1) At matched $\fvu \approx 0.49$, the SAE transmits 32
coefficients where the Ontologizer needs 320 (a 10$\times$
coefficient-efficiency gap), with the caveat that top-$\nu$ points are
post-hoc truncations of a model never trained to truncate. (2) The
Ontologizer's granularity floor (one deviation per head, 160 coefficients,
$\fvu\,0.677$) is worse than the SAE's 32-coefficient point --- the very
sparse regime is structurally closed to it. (3) The parameter- and
coefficient-matched dense control (\texttt{k5120}) reaches
$\fvu\,1.1\times10^{-4}$ with $3.4\times$ fewer active coefficients than
the soft Ontologizer: unstructured reconstruction is $\sim$50$\times$
better at matched budgets. (4) Head-level codes (mean-ablate all but a
layer's lowest-entropy heads) are far weaker than deviation codes at matched
coefficients (heads $8/32$: $0.50$ vs.\ dev $\nu{=}8$: $0.094$):
reconstruction information is spread near-evenly across all 160 heads ---
under the classifier reading, every head's assignment carries signal for
every input, which is what a MoEfied code should look like, and why
per-sample head \emph{confidence} does not localize reconstruction.
(5) Uniform-origin truncation is strictly worse than
$\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{p}]$-origin (dev $\nu{=}1$: $0.98$ vs.\ $0.68$): the measured
mean classification, not uniform, is the code's true origin; neutral
ablations and steering baselines should pin to it. (6) The soft model's
own discrete reading is not a code at all. The argmax row is 77$\times$
above the Gaussian reference for 800 bits ($0.226$), the least distortion
an 800-bit code could reach if the embeddings were Gaussian with their
measured covariance; real embeddings are no harder to compress than that,
so the argmax is at least 77$\times$ from optimal
(Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}, Equation~\ref{eq:ratefloor}). On the
selection sweep's \texttt{softmax\_shm} run the gap is nine orders of
magnitude (argmax $\fvu$ $8.7\times10^6$ against $0.0007$ soft). The
entries that \texttt{decode.py}'s interventions and \texttt{decode\_tags.py}
read are therefore not what carries a softmax model's embedding --- the
motivation for Section~\ref{sec:ste}. The reference does not say that 800
bits are too few: non-Gaussian data can be compressed below it, so it
informs the larger head count of Section~\ref{sec:ste} without requiring
it.

The per-head simplex code constraint alone costs $\sim$30$\times$ vs.\ the
unstructured dense control ($0.0044$ vs.\ $1.1\times10^{-4}$); against
\texttt{resid\_nc}, everything the Ontologizer adds beyond rung 2b ---
bilinear classifiers, five residual layers, the shared 2048-d dictionary
space --- adds \emph{nothing} to reconstruction (slightly negative), at
$2.2\times$ the parameters. That verdict is specific to the reference
checkpoint: the selection sweep's \texttt{softmax\_shm} run, identical in
shape, reaches $0.0007$ on the same tail (in-sample, like
\texttt{resid\_nc}), six times below \texttt{g160softmax}. The stack can
therefore beat its single-layer rival on reconstruction; whether it does
so out of sample is open. Hard per-head selection costs only $\sim$6\%
over an unstructured code of equal sparsity (\texttt{g160top1} 0.274
vs.\ \texttt{k160} 0.258), which also shows the Ontologizer's
catastrophic argmax code ($\fvu\,17.3$; one-hot selection inflates output
norm $3.7\times$ because winners' atoms carry $\sim$4$\times$ the
soft-mixture norm) is a training artifact, not intrinsic to discrete head
codes --- Section~\ref{sec:ste} confirms this from the Ontologizer side.
Nested prefix losses are nearly free. The bilinear encoder costs
$\sim$20\% $\fvu$ and kills a third of the dictionary. Under the steering
frame, the ladder's implication is pointed: \emph{the classifier-forming
ingredient is per-head softmax itself}; whether the full stack earns its
extra cost must be decided by task naturalness and steering quality, not
reconstruction.

Categorical structure exists precisely when it is trained in as hard
selection: \texttt{g160top1}'s partition is perfectly head-like, while
soft per-head codes converge to dense near-uniform mixtures and
spontaneous SAE group structure is co-firing topical clusters
(Appendix~\ref{sec:categorical}). The rest of the softmax-versus-SAE
battery --- feature reliability, text fidelity, language localization and
shrinkage --- is in Appendix~\ref{app:soft}.

\label{sec:prelim-orth}

k32 discovered (\texttt{m11264}) & 359 & $+1.7$ & 0.36 & 0.02 \\

g160softmax (trained, soft)   & 160 & $-14.7$ & 0.00 & \textbf{1.00} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Within-head decode-direction coherence vs.\ size-matched
same-pool nulls (\texttt{headcoh.py}, $n_{\mathrm{null}}{=}500$).}
\end{table}

This is the sharpest dissociation in the battery, and the one the
steering goal predicts. Hard-trained heads are hugely \emph{coherent}
(every \texttt{g160top1} head far above null: winner-take-all carves the
data so each head's atoms tile one decoder cluster --- fine-grained
substitutions within a region). Post-hoc co-fire clusters are mildly
coherent (topical). But both soft classifier architectures are
\emph{anti-coherent}: in 96--100\% of heads, the entries' decode
directions are \emph{more mutually dissimilar than random same-pool
sets}. Softmax competition pushes a head's atoms apart ---
within-head entries are well-separated alternatives, i.e.\ heads function
as \emph{contrastive classifiers}, and within-head swaps are large, clean
moves along the head's own dimension. Notably the signature is shared by
the Ontologizer and single-layer \texttt{g160softmax} with near-identical
strength. The choice of inner product is not doing the work: recomputed
under the whitened metric the training objective actually optimizes, the
repulsion \emph{strengthens} for the Ontologizer (mean $\zeta_{\cos}$
$-24.4$ vs.\ $-17.6$ raw; frac $\zeta{<}{-}2$ unchanged at $0.96$) and is
essentially unchanged for \texttt{g160softmax} ($-14.7$) and
\texttt{g160top1} ($+179.3$ vs.\ $+161.3$).

The hard-code Ontologizer agrees from the weights side. Its within-head
row cosine, decoded into output space, is $0.023$ at layer 0 and $-0.015$
to $-0.020$ at layers 1--4: a head's decoded atoms are essentially orthogonal,
and slightly repelling, in the space that matters. The same statistic read
in the $e$-dimensional dictionary space instead reads $0.12$--$0.29$; that
reading is dominated by the decoder's null space and should not be used
(Appendix~\ref{sec:rowcos}). Of the two orthogonalities in
Section~\ref{sec:disjoint}, then, the within-head one holds in both code
types. \textbf{The between-head one does not}: no trained model partitions
its dictionary space between heads. The support overlap $\omega$ sits at
or above a null that shuffles each head's coordinates, and each head's
usage covers 52--98\% of the coordinates where a partition into 32 heads
needs about 3\% (Appendix~\ref{sec:support}). Heads are contrast sets that
share their coordinates.

Two \texttt{resid\_nc} outlier heads (layer-2 h25, $\zeta{=}{+}346$; layer-3
h28, $\zeta{=}{+}280$; the next candidates sit in the partially frozen layer 4)
cluster instead of contrasting --- their sampled entries also decode to
generic text, so we suspect partial head collapse (entries never
differentiated), not semantic coherence; targeted inspection pending.
The description-text view was uninformative on plain summaries
($\sim$77\% generic corpus descriptions for entries, 49\% for k32 latents,
inflating the permutation null: pooled $\zeta{=}{-}0.25$); re-run on the
contrastive \texttt{cacts} descriptions it acquires signal. Within-head
description similarity clears the head-membership permutation null for
\texttt{g160top1} ($\zeta{=}{+}2.42$, 62 within-head pairs), is marginal for
Ontologizer heads ($\zeta{=}{+}1.92$, 155 pairs), and is absent for
\texttt{g160softmax} ($\zeta{=}{+}0.79$, 217 pairs). Read against the
geometry this is the pattern ``alternative values of one shared
dimension'' predicts: Ontologizer entries' decode \emph{directions}
repel while their described \emph{contents} weakly cohere. It is also
the first instrument in the battery to separate the Ontologizer from
its single-layer soft rival, whose head contents show no description
coherence at all --- suggestive rather than significant at this
coverage (one global $\zeta$ per model over the campaign's sampled
features).

\subsection{Steering fidelity}
\label{sec:steer}

Steering through the text round trip (decode the steered embedding,
re-encode, read the code) is reported in
Appendix~\ref{sec:steertext}: steerability there does not track
reconstruction, but an unsteered round trip preserves a head's argmax on
only $\sim$12\% of rows, so the text cycle cannot resolve an intervention
and the measurement below is taken in embedding space instead.
\texttt{steerembed.py} removes the round trip:

\textbf{Steering works, and the text cycle was hiding it.} The decode
direction realizes the target entry on 82\% of rows for the softmax model
at strength 0.25, where the text cycle put every model within noise of
random. \textbf{The hard code is the less steerable of the two}: softmax
realizes 0.82 at collateral 0.58, where the hard code realizes 0.36
while already disturbing more (0.66) --- a hard argmax has margins a
small nudge cannot cross. Sweeping strength on the hard code, its decode
direction saturates at $\sim$0.65 (a third of interventions are
unrealizable at any strength), and it overtakes the soft model only past
collateral 0.85, where most other heads have moved too.

\textbf{\texttt{BilinearBlock.rev} had a sign bug}: \texttt{eigh} fixes
eigenvectors only up to sign, so \texttt{rev} returned a direction whose
sign came from LAPACK. \texttt{nlinear.orient} now fixes it by the
eigenvalue's sign with a leading-coordinate tie-break. The fix roughly
doubles eigenvector steering on the soft model (0.224 $\to$ 0.427 at
0.25), within noise of orienting per sample (0.446). That is more than
theory allows: moving $\mathbf{x}$ by $\alpha$ along $\mathbf{v}$ changes
$\mathbf{x}^\top B\mathbf{x}$ by $2\alpha\lambda (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{v})$,
a property of the sample that a weight-only
convention should not be able to supply. It can here because SONAR is
anisotropic --- $\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{v}$ is dominated by
$\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}]\cdot\mathbf{v}$, which
has a consistent sign --- whereas on isotropic Gaussian inputs (the unit
test) a weight-only direction raises the logit on about half the samples.
On the hard code the oriented eigendirection is dead, below random at
every strength. The depth decomposition of steering, which reverses the aggregate ranking of
\texttt{decode} and \texttt{grad}, is in Appendix~\ref{sec:steerlayer}.

At each method's own step size the difference of means realizes more in
most models, partly by taking longer steps

model's own categories; steering an external concept, against
supervised vectors fitted to that concept's labels, is the comparison
still to run, along with per-head effect consistency across contexts.

The existence proof from \texttt{resid\_nc}
stands: forcing (L1,h8,k17) reliably yields Burmese decodes of otherwise
preserved content.

\subsection{Description specificity (auto-interp)}

Under the identical plain summarization prompt, 51\% of \texttt{k32}
latents receive a specific description (20\% naming a language or
script) versus 23\% of Ontologizer entries (8\%): individual SAE latents
are about twice as describable as individual entries, consistent with
entries not being standalone features. Blinded detection over the full campaign
agrees, and sharpens the reading:

Three readings. (1) \emph{Per-latent describability tracks code density,
not architecture.} Activation-based descriptions rank
\texttt{k32}${\approx}$\texttt{g160top1} (0.65) $>$ \texttt{k32\_bl}
(0.58) $>$ onto ${\approx}$ \texttt{k5120} (0.47) $>$
\texttt{g160softmax} (0.45). Pearson(log nominal density, \texttt{acts})
is $-0.894$ across the six runs, so density alone carries $\sim$80\% of
the variance. Fitting \texttt{cacts} linearly in $\log(\text{firing rate})$
on the four \texttt{sae.py} runs with nonzero firing rate (slope
$-0.069$, Pearson $-0.766$) and holding the Ontologizer out predicts $0.421$
against an actual $0.392$, a residual of $-1.8$ sem: the Ontologizer sits
on the trend. \texttt{g160top1} is the real outlier, $+0.120$ above it ---
the Ontologizer's categorical code in single-layer form, and unusually
describable for its density. The fit is thin (four runs, weak correlation;
\texttt{g160softmax} is excluded because its thresholded firing rate is
exactly 0, and the $2/k$ threshold is non-monotone in true density), but
the lesson is durable: \textbf{per-latent detection F1 cannot compare
architectures at unmatched density, and every soft Ontologizer is dense
by construction.} (2) \emph{The contrastive mode is the honest number.}
\texttt{cacts} scores below \texttt{acts} everywhere, but the drop is
smallest for the sparse rungs ($-$0.04 to $-$0.05) and largest for the
dense control ($-$0.14): part of every plain-summary score is
generic-match inflation, and the contrastive prompt removes most of it.
(3) \emph{Parameter-space decodes are not descriptions.} \texttt{params}
is ${\le}0.06$ everywhere except plain \texttt{k32} (0.158), and the
bilinear eigenfeature decode fails identically (\texttt{eig} 0.021). Part
of that is a decoding error: an atom's cosine to its head's mean atom is
$0.849$, so a raw decode returns one shared template sentence with
details shuffled, and anything resting on \texttt{decode\_tags.py} reads
the template rather than the feature. Subtracting the origin decode
(\texttt{pdev}) makes the decodes legible but not detectable --- on one
head, \texttt{pdev} scores $0.052$ against a $0.014$ null, \texttt{params}
$0.031$ against $0.000$, and \texttt{cacts} $0.479$ against $0.273$. A
decoded sentence is an \emph{instance} of what an entry writes, and
detection needs a criterion. Naming requires activation evidence.

\section{A hard-code arm: straight-through selection}
\label{sec:ste}

The softmax model's discrete reading is not its code
(Section~\ref{sec:pareto}, finding (6)), so its argmax entries are not
what interventions act on. Under \texttt{select="ste"} the forward pass
\emph{is} the argmax and no such gap can open: at every checkpoint scored,
the hard and soft-forward rows of \texttt{pareto.py} agree to four
decimals.

\subsection{Capacity, and why the head count rises}

A hard code transmits exactly $l\cdot h\log_2 k$ index bits. The bits a
code of this shape would need to reach a given error \emph{if the
embeddings were Gaussian} (the reference of Equation~\ref{eq:ratefloor};
real embeddings may need fewer):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrl@{}}
\toprule
$\fvu$ target & bits & heads at $k{=}32$, $l{=}5$ \\
\midrule
0.226 & 800 & 160 (the softmax architecture) \\
0.05 & 1{,}898 & 380 (this arm, $h{=}76$) \\
0.0023 & 4{,}167 & 833 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Heads are the cheap axis: bits and parameters are both linear in $h$, and
heads run in parallel where layers are sequential. The arm is $5\times76$,
$k{=}32$, 1900 bits, 51.93M parameters.

Making the classifier train under a hard forward takes three settings,
all following from the argmax being scale-invariant so that nothing
pushes the logits up: relative logit noise (\texttt{--noise-k
batchnorm}), a surrogate temperature matched to the logit scale, and a
rescaled $s_{\mathrm{KL}_m}$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:calib}). Every arm's
configuration is in Table~\ref{tab:cfg-ste-sonar}.

\subsection{Initialization: a third of the error}

and its output scales with $h$; the per-layer gain compounds that, so the
initial residual grows geometrically with depth, by a factor proportional
to $h$ per layer. The shipped arm starts at
$1.84\times10^8$ where it should start near 1.
\texttt{--dict-init-scale 0.1} rescales each dictionary so a layer
contributes about a tenth of its input, which divides $h$ out and starts
the cascade as a near-identity:

\subsection{Depth at fixed capacity}
\label{sec:depth}

Five layers of 76 heads and one layer of 380 have the same nominal
capacity (1900 bits) and exactly the same parameter count (51.93M; both
the dictionary $hke$ and the bilinear classifier $2hkd$ depend on $l$ and $h$
only through $l\cdot h$). At the fixed initialization every head in both
uses its entries almost uniformly (summed head entropy 99.7\% and 99.9\%
of nominal), and \textbf{depth is worth $1.79\times$ the reconstruction
error} ($0.1535$ vs.\ $0.2750$). Summed head entropy is an upper bound on
what a code carries; whether the two carry different amounts jointly ---
the stack's heads are conditioned on one another's errors, the flat arm's
are not --- is not measured. Seed does not explain the gap: replicas agree
to four digits. Neither arm has converged, and the flat arm is still
improving faster ($-2.75\%$ held out over its last quarter, against
$-0.86\%$ for the stack), so the ratio, which peaked at $1.83\times$ near
step 200k, is narrowing slowly; the stack leads at every checkpoint
(Appendix~\ref{sec:depthdetail}).

What depth buys is not usage, input conditioning, or the per-layer gain
channel: a ZCA-whitened flat arm uses every entry uniformly and still reconstructs
$1.7\times$ worse, and pinning the gains costs the stack only 2.1\%
(Appendix~\ref{sec:depthdetail}). Blending each layer's subtracted
reconstruction toward another row's, without retraining, shows that the
pairing of a residual with its own sample is load-bearing well below full
severance. \textbf{The cascade's value is the pairing; the conditioning is
its shadow.} A flat layer is a product quantizer --- no head can see
another's error --- while the residual stack is a residual quantizer, each
stage coding what earlier stages missed.

\subsection{Entanglement: the cost of the cascade}
\label{sec:entangle}

\textbf{A single-head embedding-space intervention in the flat model
realizes its target on almost every row (99.6\%) while disturbing 33\% of
the other heads; in the stack it realizes 37\% while disturbing 66\%.}
Restricting the stack to layer 0, so the target is the same distance from
the input, widens the gap (realized / collateral; strength 0.25, and the
model's native step for the last row; 12 layer-0 targets in the stack, 64
in the flat arm):

\textbf{Collateral is not a property of the intervention.} In both models
every direction disturbs as many other heads as a random step of the same
length, to within 0.05. Neither model's heads are independently
addressable: the hard code's heads sit almost on their decision
boundaries, with a median winner--runner-up logit margin of
$1.5\times10^{-4}$ at layer 0 and in the flat arm, so a step of a quarter
of the input's norm crosses many of them. Linearizing each head's logits
with the upstream codes held fixed (\texttt{steergeom.py}) separates the
two paths: the stack's layer-0 heads flip directly about as often as the
flat arm's (0.22 against 0.24 to first order), so what the stack adds is
downstream layers reclassifying a changed residual. \textbf{The cascade
roughly doubles collateral; it is not the only source of it.} At the
model's own scale --- the forced entry's output change, without
rescaling --- the flat arm's step is small (median 0.028 of $\lVert
\mathbf{x}\rVert$) and moves 4\% of the other heads, while the stack's (0.13)
realizes 8\% of targets across layers and moves 54\%, exactly what a
random step of that length does. This is the steering-side counterpart of
\texttt{compose.py}'s finding that non-additivity decays with the number
of layers below an intervention (Section~\ref{sec:formal}). Steering by target layer, the
head-health levers tried against it (of which only a row-collinearity
setpoint bought steerability), and the head-independence penalty that
turned out to be measuring estimator bias are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:steerlayer} and~\ref{sec:hsic}.

\textbf{What reproduces is aggregate or layer-level; what identifies an

Layer 0 is the exception in every head-level measure, consistent with it

for other heads. The softmax Ontologizer's partitions fare no better: a
seed replica at matched training matches at most 0.1\% of entries, where
the k32 SAE pair matches 3--31\% (Figure~\ref{fig:softseed}).

\subsubsection{What layer 0's lead is made of}

Three findings, detailed in Appendix~\ref{sec:l0size}
and~\ref{sec:entries}. \emph{Layer 0's larger contributions are all
gain}: it receives the raw input, while its atoms are no larger than
layer 1's (Table~\ref{tab:gainatoms}); at equal atom spread it still
reproduces $14\times$ better on GPT-2 and $23\times$ on SONAR. \emph{The
stack costs reproducibility at matched size, at every layer}: against
flat-arm heads of the same size, which see the same input with nothing
downstream, the stack's layer 0 reproduces $\sim$8$\times$ worse and its
deeper layers 19--49$\times$ worse --- the reproducibility face of
entanglement. \emph{Within layer 0 the atoms decide}: layer-0 entries are
unequal by atom norm rather than usage (Table~\ref{tab:entries}), and on
SONAR one head (54) holds 8 of the layer's 11 atoms above three times the
median, reproduces at 0.844 against 0.249 for the next, and drives the
within-layer correlation between agreement and largest atom
(Table~\ref{tab:bigatoms}); without it the layer-0 mean falls only from
0.080 to 0.070. GPT-2's layer 0 has no such head: every head carries a
graded tail of large atoms and agreement is narrowly spread.

\subsection{Heads against a known factor: language, and what they encode instead}
\label{sec:lang}

Language is the one factor of the corpus with free labels, which is why it
is measured here --- not because a head was expected to encode it. SONAR
is trained to be language-agnostic, mapping translations to nearby
points, so language should be a weak, residual factor of the embedding,
and a reconstruction objective has little reason to spend a head on it.
The measurement is a probe of whether head partitions line up with a
known factor at all, and of how much of one a head can carry.

As expected, no head encodes language outright: the best reaches a
partition NMI of 0.18 against an attainable 0.875 (0.165 on the second
seed), and no head-health lever changes that
(Table~\ref{tab:headlang}). Partitions do carry the factor well above
chance, and \textbf{the hard code carries more of it}: four times the
softmax reference on null-corrected best-head NMI, and 0.42 against 0.28
in a 32-head probe. Single cells and conjunctions of cells detect language
\emph{neighbourhood} rather than identity --- Finnish with Estonian,
Japanese with Korean --- which matches the embedding's geometry, where
languages are weakly separated regions rather than directions
(Appendix~\ref{sec:langdetail}).

\paragraph{What the strongest head holds: topic and genre.} What a
semantic, language-agnostic embedding should be organized by is meaning,
and its strongest head is. \texttt{topichead.py} ranks layer-0 heads by
$\eta^2$ and matches partitions across runs (65{,}536 held-out rows):

contributions (0.844 against 0.249 for the next;
Appendix~\ref{sec:entries}), the exception to Section~\ref{sec:seeds}'s

same-run control it is the same variable carved differently, not the same
partition. It mixes languages freely (NMI 0.155). Decoded as deviations
from the head mean, its entries read as topic and genre --- crime news,

manufacturing, marketing, legislative text, entertainment. Contrastive

natural variables therefore wants topic or genre labels, which this
corpus does not carry.

\section{Hard codes on GPT-2 small: atoms in activation space}
\label{sec:gpt2hard}

A second line of straight-through runs (\texttt{experiments/gpt2}, from the
\texttt{headline} branch) takes the hard code to GPT-2 small and adds the
measure an embedding substrate lacks: the fraction of GPT-2's loss
recovered when the reconstruction is spliced back into the model,
$(\mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{ablate}} - \mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{recon}})
/(\mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{ablate}} - \mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{clean}})$ with the
residual mean-ablated as the floor.

\paragraph{Setup, and what is not comparable.} Activations are the residual
stream \emph{entering} block 8 (Section~\ref{sec:seeds}'s GPT-2 runs use the
stream leaving it), mean-centred and scaled to unit mean square, on
FineWeb; $\fvu$ here is unwhitened. These numbers therefore do not compare
with the other GPT-2 results in this document, only with each other and
with the SAE baselines trained on the same cache. Held-out evaluation uses
documents disjoint from training: 520k tokens for the 40M-token runs, 65k
for the one-epoch ones. The runs use three options this branch does not
implement --- \texttt{direct}, \texttt{resid\_first} and \texttt{gain\_clip},
below --- so they reproduce only on \texttt{headline}; its private heads are
this branch's \texttt{ConcatDictBlock}, and its fibers and head dropout
have been ported.

\paragraph{The design.} Relative to Section~\ref{sec:ste}, three changes.
\emph{Direct atoms}: dictionary atoms are signed vectors in activation
space itself, with no latent dictionary space and no decoder, so every
atom is literally a steering direction. \emph{Gain-shape on every layer}:
layer 0 too classifies the unit direction of what it corrects
(\texttt{resid\_first}), and each layer's contribution is scaled by the
observed residual norm, clipped at $\lVert \mathbf{x}\rVert$, which makes the model
positively homogeneous, $f(\xi\mathbf{x}) = \xi f(\mathbf{x})$ for $\xi > 0$.
\emph{Annealed straight-through
selection}: argmax forward, softmax backward, with the temperature
annealed early in training (Table~\ref{tab:cfg-gpt2-headline}). With
$5\times32$ heads of 32 entries the code is 800 bits per token with no
continuous coefficients.

\section{Dictionary geometry and the non-negativity constraint}
\label{sec:geom}

Two geometric facts frame this section and are detailed in
Appendix~\ref{app:geom}. Row collinearity measured in the $e$-dimensional
dictionary space is mostly gauge: 74--78\% of each atom's energy lies in
the decoder's null space, so dictionary statistics should be computed
after decoding (Appendix~\ref{sec:rowcos}). And the selection rule shapes
the dictionary less than training duration does, while the last layer
becomes inert once the error before it is small
(Appendix~\ref{sec:lastlayer}).

\subsection{The orthant forces a collinear dictionary}
\label{sec:gpt2}

Non-negativity binds through the Gram, not the null space: two
non-negative vectors have $\langle \mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}'\rangle\geq0$ exactly, so for
independent $\lvert\mathcal{N}(0,1)\rvert$ coordinates the chance pairwise
cosine is $2/\pi = 0.637$, with minimum $+0.58$ over 4000 draws; signed
draws give 0.000. \texttt{--signed} drops the \texttt{abs()} in
\texttt{DictBlock.dicts}. On GPT-2 layer 8 with the straight-through arm's
shape, crossed with dictionary width, at convergence (371{,}900 steps;
geometry from \texttt{dictgeom.py}, $\fvu$ held out and scored from each
checkpoint):

\textbf{The forced collinearity costs reconstruction.} At $e{=}1536$,
\texttt{abs} runs at a third of the signed effective rank and 26\% more
held-out error; at $e{=}768$, 90\% more. Across the five arms $\bar\kappa$ orders
the error exactly at convergence (Spearman $+1.00$; $+0.90$ at step 90k,
where concat is the single inversion). The cost is also an
\emph{interaction}: narrowing $e$ costs $+0.2\%$ signed and $+51\%$ under
\texttt{abs}, so the wide dictionary buys room for the constraint, not
representational capacity --- which undercuts Section~\ref{sec:disjoint}'s
claim that head-disjointness is what $e > d$ buys, and no model attains
head-disjointness in any case (Appendix~\ref{sec:support}). \texttt{abs} is worse
than signed at both widths, and its gap at $e{=}1536$ grew
from 8\% at step 20k to 50\% at 200k and has since narrowed to 26\%
(Figure~\ref{fig:orthant-curves}), so \texttt{abs} may be partly slower,
but it has not caught up by the end of a full run. A geometric reading via
Wendel's theorem~\cite{wendel1962}: the decoder's $e$ random columns lie in
a common halfspace with probability $2^{1-e}\sum_{q<d}\binom{e-1}{q}$,
exactly $1/2$ at $e{=}2d$, so $e{=}1536$ sits on the threshold where a random
decoder's cone $\{W_{\mathrm{dec}}\mathbf{r} : \mathbf{r}\geq0\}$ covers the
output space at all.

\label{fig:orthant-curves}
\end{figure}

Removing \texttt{abs} therefore buys both: a decorrelated,
near-full-effective-rank dictionary and lower error, which is roughly what
the collinearity setpoint exists to buy. Signed atoms give up two things:
subtractive features are harder to read as ``this concept is present'', and
the $\lVert F\rVert_1 \geq h$ floor under \texttt{norm\_rows}, which rests
on non-cancellation, disappears. Concatenated heads, which face a collapsed
tradeoff rather than a raised floor, are the worst arm
(Appendix~\ref{sec:concatgeom}).
